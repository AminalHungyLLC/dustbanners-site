Part 1

The Game

Dustbanners is a two-player card game set on a broken frontier, about eighty years after the world fell apart. You lead a Banner into a valley of nine places. Every faction flies a banner, and in this game the side you lead is called a Banner.

How it plays

  • 2 players, ages 14 and up.
  • Two ways to win, and both are always open: break the enemy Leader, or hold all 9 places at the end of a turn.
  • Each player draws 5 opening cards, and both players start their first turn with 3 Supply.
  • The Frontier Deck turns up something every turn (a storm, a wild animal, a find) that both of you have to deal with.

Two sides that play nothing alike

The first box has two factions: the Wardens and the Raiders. Each Banner has two Leaders and two Companions to choose from. More factions to come.

Wardens

“How do we protect what’s ours?”

Their word is Guard. A Guard protects your things where it stands: your Units, Companions and Leader there, and the place itself if you hold it.

Raiders

“What can we take?”

Their word is Ambush. Its card words are still being tested, so they are not shown yet.

What’s in it for you

  • A world that will grow. Each season will add a chapter of the story, and new cards will come from it.
  • The same box for everyone. No random packs. Every box has the same cards, and nothing stronger is sold later.
  • Tested before you play. More than 17,000 simulated games so far, computer against computer, to help balance the cards.
  • Made by people. AI tools help me build the software, test balance and draft text; the ideas and decisions are mine. The card and board pictures shown now are AI placeholders, labeled. Human artists will make the final art, card backs and logo, and a human editor will do the final text pass.

If you like…

  • Magic: The Gathering, but want fixed boxes, a simpler resource (we call it Supply), and a world whose story grows with every season.
  • Android: Netrunner, for two sides that play differently, no random packs, and a frontier with its own history to read.
  • KeyForge, for decks that are ready to play out of the box.
  • Marvel Champions, for fixed expansions instead of boosters, each one a new chapter of the story.
  • Root, for factions that feel nothing alike, each with its own people, places and sayings.
  • Arcs, for card-driven strategy in a world you can read as deep as you like.

Those games belong to their makers. Dustbanners is not connected to any of them.

The cards

Every card in the first box is one of these eight kinds. Here is one of each, with its real rules text. The names are plain stand-ins for now. When the AI art is on, the pictures in the cards are AI-made placeholders. Use the “AI art” button at the top to swap them for empty boxes. The final art is coming from human artists.

  • Leader

    Placeholder art (AI-made). Final art by human artists.

    Your Leader leads your Banner. If your Leader is defeated, you lose.

  • Companion

    Placeholder art (AI-made). Final art by human artists.

    A Companion is an animal or a person who fights beside your Leader. If it is defeated, you can bring it back.

  • Unit

    Placeholder art (AI-made). Final art by human artists.

    Units are your Banner’s people. They move, attack, defend and capture places.

  • Tactic

    Placeholder art (AI-made). Final art by human artists.

    A Tactic is a one-time action. Do what it says, then put it in your discard pile, the Ditch.

  • Equipment

    Placeholder art (AI-made). Final art by human artists.

    Equipment is gear. It goes on a card it names, or it sits at a place you control.

  • Structure

    Placeholder art (AI-made). Final art by human artists.

    A Structure is something you build at a place you control.

  • Battlefield Location

    Placeholder art (AI-made). Final art by human artists.

    A Location is one of the nine places on the map. Hold all 9 at the end of a turn to win.

  • Frontier card

    Placeholder art (AI-made). Final art by human artists.

    Frontier cards come from the shared deck. Storms, finds, animals and trouble happen to both of you.

About

Made by people

The creator

I’m Kyle. I make games as AminalHungy, a one-person studio in Colorado. I grew up on Sierra adventures, SimCity, Fallout and early Magic: The Gathering.

Dustbanners began on a hike in Boulder: my friend Ross and I climbed a trail, and the next morning I woke up wanting to build the world I had seen from near the top. It is a two-player card game for people who like to plan with a deck and then get surprised by one they can only guess.

How AI is used

The finished game will be made by people: the art by paid human artists, the final words by a paid human editor.

I use AI tools to help make Dustbanners. The world, the rules and every final decision are mine. AI tools helped build and test the digital version of the game, run its balance simulations, and draft some of the text, which I edit and approve. A paid human editor will do the final pass on every word players see. The pictures on the cards, the board and the game screens shown now are AI-made placeholder art, and they are labeled. The final card art, box art, card backs and logo will be made by paid human artists. AI art is used only for our own placeholders.

Are you an artist? Sign up to draw for Dustbanners. It will be paid work once the game is funded.
