The game in three lines

  1. Two players. Nine places.Ages 14 and up. Each of you leads a faction into the valley.
  2. Two ways to winBoth are always open: break the enemy Leader, or hold all 9 places at the end of a turn.
  3. Trouble every turnThe Frontier Deck turns up something every turn (a storm, a wild animal, a find) that both of you have to deal with.

Part 1

The Game

You lead a Banner into a valley of nine places, about eighty years after the world fell apart. Every faction flies a banner, and in this game the side you lead is called a Banner.

How it plays

Each player draws 5 opening cards, and both players start their first turn with 3 Supply.

Two sides that play nothing alike

The first box has two factions: the Wardens and the Raiders. Each Banner has two Leaders and two Companions to choose from. More factions to come.

Wardens

“How do we protect what’s ours?”

Their word is Guard. A Guard protects your things where it stands: your Units, Companions and Leader there, and the place itself if you hold it.

Raiders

“What can we take?”

Their word is Ambush. An Ambush card waits for the moment written on it, then strikes.

What’s in it for you

  • A world that will grow. Each season will add a chapter of the story, and new cards will come from it.
  • The same box for everyone. No random packs. Every box has the same cards, and nothing stronger is sold later.
  • Tested before you play. More than 20,000 simulated games so far, computer against computer, to help balance the cards.
  • Made by people. The ideas and decisions are mine. Human artists will make the final art, card backs and logo, and a human editor will do the final text pass.

If you like…

  • Magic: The Gathering, but want fixed boxes, a simpler resource (we call it Supply), and a world whose story grows with every season.
  • Android: Netrunner, for two sides that play differently, no random packs, and a frontier with its own history to read.
  • KeyForge, for decks that are ready to play out of the box.
  • Root, for factions that feel nothing alike, each with its own people, places and sayings.

Those games belong to their makers. Dustbanners is not connected to any of them.

The cards

Every card in the first box is one of these nine kinds. Pick a Banner to see one of each kind, with its real rules text. The names are plain stand-ins for now. The pictures are my own photos, and a plain box means that card has no photo yet. The final art will come from human artists.

On a card: the number in the circle is its cost in Supply. ATK is Attack, RET is Retaliation, DUR is Durability and CAP is Capacity.

Words on these cards

  • Fighter: a Unit or Companion. A card that also means your Leader says “fighter or Leader”.
  • Fortify N: prevent the next N damage to that card this turn, unless the card says longer. That card is the one the line names, or the one damage is about to hit. It can be any card you control. If Fortify happens because damage is about to hit, it stops only that damage.
  • Momentum: it gets +1 Attack through your next turn, even if it moves. Momentum from different cards adds up. One card’s Momentum doesn’t add to itself.
  • Rethink: draw 1, then discard 1.

About

Made by people

The creator

I’m Kyle. I make games as AminalHungy, a one-person studio in Colorado. I grew up on Sierra adventures, SimCity, Fallout and early Magic: The Gathering.

Dustbanners began on a hike in Boulder: my friend Ross and I climbed a trail, and the next morning I woke up wanting to build the world I had seen from near the top. It is a two-player card game for people who like to plan with a deck and then get surprised by one they can only guess.

How AI is used

The finished game will be made by people: the art by paid human artists, the final words by a paid human editor.

I use AI tools to help make Dustbanners. The world, the rules and every final decision are mine. AI tools helped build and test the digital version of the game, run its balance simulations, and draft some of the text, which I edit and approve. A paid human editor will do the final pass on every word players see. The card pictures on this site are my own photos. I adjusted them with simple editing scripts. No AI image generator made or changed them. Where a card has no photo yet, you see a plain labeled box. The final card art, box art, card backs and logo will be made by paid human artists.

Are you an artist? Sign up to draw for Dustbanners. It will be paid work once the game is funded.
